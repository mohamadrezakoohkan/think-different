# The Maximum-Pressure Prompt

How to put **all eleven crazy ones** on one problem, as hard as they can push.

`think-different` normally runs a short arc of three roles. It switches to all eleven only when you ask for it explicitly: say **"full arc"**, **"all eleven roles"**, or **"the full treatment"**. The brief is where the pressure comes from: each field you fill in gives a specific role something concrete to work against.

## The short way

```text
Give me the full treatment, all eleven roles: think different about why 40% of our trial users quit before day 3.
```

## The maximum-pressure way

```text
/think-different full arc — all eleven roles, maximum pressure.

CHALLENGE: 40% of trial users abandon our B2B scheduling tool before day 3.

CONTEXT:
- Trial is 14 days, card not required. Activation = first shared calendar.
- Median time-to-activation is 4 days; activated users convert at 31%.
- Read: ./docs/onboarding-flow.md, ./data/trial-cohorts.csv
- Competitors all copy the same 5-step setup wizard.

DECISION: Q3 roadmap lock on Oct 20 — this decides where 2 engineers go.

CURRENT PLAN: Add onboarding emails on days 1, 2 and 3, plus a progress checklist.

HARD LIMITS: GDPR, no dark patterns, no auto-charging cards.

AUDIENCE & FORMAT: A 90-second pitch to our CEO, who has rejected "more emails" twice.

PRESSURE:
- Send any agent back once if its output is thin or obvious.
- Pause after the Rebel, Misfit and Troublemaker so I can redirect.
- Keep the different idea whole; show the safe option separately.
- After the synthesis, red-team the winning idea once more with think-troublemaker.
```

## Blank template

```text
/think-different full arc — all eleven roles, maximum pressure.

CHALLENGE: <the problem in one sentence>

CONTEXT:
- <facts and numbers>
- Read: <paths to docs or data>
- <what everyone in your field already does>

DECISION: <what this feeds, and by when>

CURRENT PLAN: <what you'd do if you changed nothing>

HARD LIMITS: <law, safety, ethics, non-negotiables>

AUDIENCE & FORMAT: <who must be convinced, and in what form>

PRESSURE:
- Send any agent back once if its output is thin or obvious.
- Pause after the Rebel, Misfit and Troublemaker so I can redirect.
- Keep the different idea whole; show the safe option separately.
- After the synthesis, red-team the winning idea once more with think-troublemaker.
```

## Which field drives which role

| You provide | Who uses it |
|---|---|
| CHALLENGE | Round Peg reframes it first; every later role then works on the reframed version |
| CONTEXT + files | All 33 agents can read them; the Seer hunts anomalies in your data |
| DECISION | Seer keeps only observations that would change this decision |
| CURRENT PLAN | Troublemaker attacks it alongside the new idea (the status quo can fail too) |
| HARD LIMITS | Rebel marks them load-bearing and never breaks them; Healer checks remedies against them |
| AUDIENCE & FORMAT | Inspirer writes the final pitch for exactly that person |
| PRESSURE | Overrides the defaults: extra checkpoints, send-backs, and one more red-team pass at the end |

## What happens

1. **The plan is announced.** All eleven roles in order: Round Peg, Seer → Rebel, Misfit, Troublemaker → Explorer, Inventor, Imaginer, Healer → Creator, Inspirer. You can redirect it there, before anything runs.
2. **Roles run one after another, with a checkpoint after each.** Each role passes only its handoff notes to the next, not its whole output. With the example's pressure lines, it stops after the Troublemaker to wait for you.
3. **About 35 agent runs in total** (three per role, up to four for the Inspirer, plus the extra red-team pass), some of them in parallel within a role. Expect a long session; that's the cost of maximum depth.
4. **What you get at the end:**
   - one different idea, with what it breaks, why it might work, what would kill it, and a first move;
   - runner-up ideas, each tagged with the role that produced it;
   - the safe option, shown separately for contrast;
   - a working prototype from the Creator;
   - the pitch from the Inspirer;
   - a final red-team report on the winning idea.

Every field is optional. If you skip some, it still runs, and it asks at most one question, only if the challenge itself is unclear. The fuller the brief, the harder each role can push.
